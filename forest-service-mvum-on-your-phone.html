How do I get a Forest Service motor vehicle use map on my phone, offline?

Checked against its sources on November 20, 2026

Short answer

Download your forest's Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM) as a PDF from the forest's own website while you have a connection, then open it in an app that reads geospatial PDFs. Your GPS position then shows on the map with no cell signal. Since November 2026 many Forest Service pages say some updated maps "may no longer be available through Avenza" and suggest searching your app store for a GeoPDF or offline PDF map app instead.

What a Motor Vehicle Use Map is

A Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM) shows the roads, trails and areas of a national forest or grassland that are open to motor vehicles, by vehicle class and by season. Forests publish them under the Forest Service's travel management rule (36 CFR 212.56), and driving off the routes they designate is prohibited (36 CFR 261.13), so the MVUM is the map that counts on the ground. Most forests reissue them every year, usually as one PDF per ranger district or area.

Many MVUM PDFs are geospatial PDFs: besides the drawing, they carry the coordinates of the map, so a phone app can put your GPS position on them. Most of the ones we have checked are, but not all: a few forests post scans or re-saved copies without the coordinates.

Why the Forest Service is pointing people to app stores

For years the Forest Service published its maps through the Avenza Maps store. In 2026 Avenza merged with Blue Marble Geographics and froze its map store, and its free plan stopped importing maps you download yourself. Forest Service pages updated in November 2026 now say that some updated maps, including MVUMs, "may no longer be available through Avenza", and suggest searching your app store for "GeoPDF", "geospatial PDF", "offline PDF maps" or "GPS PDF maps". The Forest Service doesn't endorse any particular app.

Put your forest's MVUM on your iPhone

  1. Find your forest's MVUM page. Search the forest's name with "motor vehicle use map", or open the forest's Maps & Publications page on fs.usda.gov. Each district usually has its own PDF; some forests post separate summer and winter maps.
  2. Download the PDF while you have a connection. On an iPhone, tap the link, then the Share button, and choose an app that reads geospatial PDFs. You can also save it to Files first.
  3. Open it in a geospatial PDF app. In Mapsheet, share the PDF to it, or tap + › From a link or QR code and paste the link from the forest's page. Mapsheet's Free maps list also includes MVUMs for many forests, checked to be geospatial before they're listed.
  4. Check that your position shows. Open the map and look for your location dot. If the app says the PDF has no map coordinates, that copy isn't geospatial: look for another version on the forest's page, or ask the forest.
  5. Check the date before you go. Use the current year's map: designations and seasons change, and the MVUM printed for this year is the one that applies.

Once the map is on your phone it works in airplane mode. GPS needs a view of the sky, not a cell signal.

Before you head out

  • Download every district you might drive through, plus the forest's visitor map or a USGS topo for the terrain the MVUM doesn't show.
  • Note the season codes in the legend: many roads are open only part of the year.
  • Phone GPS is usually good to several metres under open sky and worse under dense canopy or in steep canyons. See how accurate phone GPS is in the field.
  • Carry a paper copy for when the battery runs out.

Frequently asked questions

Is the MVUM free?

Yes. Forests publish their MVUMs as free downloads, and printed copies are free at ranger district offices.

Do I need cell service to see where I am?

No. Once the PDF is on your phone, the app uses the phone's GPS, which works with no signal and in airplane mode.

Can I still use Avenza Maps?

Avenza still opens maps already in it, but its free plan no longer imports maps you download yourself. See switching from Avenza Maps.

Why doesn't my MVUM show my position?

The copy you downloaded has no map coordinates (it isn't a geospatial PDF). Some forests post both a geospatial copy and a plain print copy; use the geospatial one.

Which app should I use?

Any app that opens geospatial PDFs (GeoPDF files) will do; the Forest Service doesn't recommend one. Mapsheet is one: free for 3 maps at a time on iPhone and iPad, no account.
